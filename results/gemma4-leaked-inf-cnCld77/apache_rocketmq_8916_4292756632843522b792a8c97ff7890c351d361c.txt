#M9S22, #E3SBJ: The "expired" lock acquisition logic in `lock()` allows a thread to proceed without actually acquiring a permit from the semaphore. However, the corresponding `unlock()` method calls `semaphore.release()`. This creates two significant issues:
1. **Permit Leak/Corruption**: If a thread "steals" the lock via the expiration path and then calls `unlock()`, it increments the semaphore's permit count without having decreased it. This can lead to the semaphore having more permits than its initial capacity, potentially allowing multiple threads into the critical section simultaneously in the future.
2. **Concurrency Violation**: By bypassing the semaphore when a lock is deemed "expired," the system allows a new thread to enter the critical section even if the previous holder is still active (e.g., due to a long GC pause or slow processing), breaking the mutual exclusion guarantee.
