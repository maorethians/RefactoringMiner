#0PTH9, #Z3EYJ: The use of a busy-wait loop (`while (!consumerLockService.tryLock(...)) {}`) to acquire the consumer lock can lead to significant CPU spikes (100% utilization on the executing core) if the lock is held for an extended period or under high contention. It is recommended to introduce a short backoff (e.g., `Thread.yield()` or a small `LockSupport.parkNanos`) or replace this with a blocking lock mechanism.

#4GRFM: In the `revive` method, there is a potential `ArrayIndexOutOfBoundsException` when calculating the backoff interval:
  ```java
  long backoffInterval = 1000L * REWRITE_INTERVALS_IN_SECONDS[
      Math.min(REWRITE_INTERVALS_IN_SECONDS.length, record.getAttemptTimes())];
  ```
  If `record.getAttemptTimes()` is equal to or greater than the length of the array (16), `Math.min` will return 16, which is an invalid index for a 16-element array. The index should be clamped to `REWRITE_INTERVALS_IN_SECONDS.length - 1`.

#JBN88: In `PopConsumerRocksdbStore`, the `deleteOptions` are configured with `setDisableWAL(true)` and `setSync(false)`. This means that deletions (ACKs) are not durable across crashes. If the broker crashes, records that were acknowledged but not yet flushed to the SST files may reappear in the store upon restart, leading to duplicate message delivery. While "at-least-once" delivery is often acceptable, this behavior should be explicitly documented as a performance trade-off.
