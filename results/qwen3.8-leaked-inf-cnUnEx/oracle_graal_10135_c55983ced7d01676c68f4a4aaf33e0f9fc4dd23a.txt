#8F8HS: The modifier order `static public` is non-idiomatic in Java. The conventional order is `public static`. While both compile identically, `public static` is the standard form used throughout the JDK and most codebases, and will be flagged by most style checkers.

#A715P, #N4H71: `stoppedCompilationTime` is a plain `long` field that is read and written from `submitForCompilation`, which can be invoked concurrently by multiple compilation threads. Without `volatile` (or equivalent synchronization), the JMM provides no visibility guarantee: one thread's write of `System.currentTimeMillis()` may not be visible to another thread's subsequent read of the `stoppedCompilationTime != 0` check. In practice the consequences are benign (a slightly delayed or duplicated retry), but declaring the field `volatile` would make the intent explicit and eliminate the data race.

#6BC19, #N4H71: `logShutdownCompilations` is a plain `boolean` used as a one-shot guard (`if (logger != null && logShutdownCompilations) { logShutdownCompilations = false; ... }`). Under concurrent invocation of `submitForCompilation`, multiple threads can all observe `true` before any of them writes `false`, causing the warning to be logged multiple times. An `AtomicBoolean` with `compareAndSet(true, false)` (or a `synchronized` block) would make the "log exactly once" guarantee hold.

#7HS2A: Catching `Throwable` here will also swallow `Error` subtypes such as `OutOfMemoryError` or `StackOverflowError`. While the intent is to be resilient against any failure from the native call and fall back to the default mode, silently swallowing `Error` can mask serious JVM-level problems and make them very hard to diagnose. Consider narrowing the catch to `Exception` (or at minimum re-throwing `Error`), or adding a comment that explicitly justifies swallowing `Error` in this context.
