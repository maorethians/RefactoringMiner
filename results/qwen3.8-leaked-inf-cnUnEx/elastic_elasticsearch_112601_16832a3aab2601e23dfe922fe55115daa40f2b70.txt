#8AEE3, #EX0P2: The constructor parameter is `int emitBatchSize` but the field is now `long`. This is a widening conversion that works, but it's inconsistent: the field was widened to `long` (presumably to pair with the `long added` in the modulo check), yet the constructor still constrains the value to `int` range. If the intent is that `emitBatchSize` could ever exceed `Integer.MAX_VALUE`, the parameter should be `long` as well (and `Math.toIntExact` in `#0H4V1` would then serve as a real guard). If it can never exceed `int`, the field could stay `int` and the modulo with `long added` would still work via implicit widening. As written, the `long` field is dead weight that may mislead readers into thinking larger values are supported.

#F0PAQ: This test performs `assertThat(add.added(), equalTo((long) i + 1))` on **every** one of the ~2.1 billion iterations, plus `assertThat(counter.count, ...)` every 5th iteration (~420 million assertions). Even at ~40 s locally, this will be a significant CI cost and the per-iteration assertion overhead likely dominates the time under test. Since the goal is to verify the `int`→`long` boundary is crossed correctly, asserting at a coarser interval (e.g., every 1 000 000 iterations) plus explicit checks at `Integer.MAX_VALUE - 1`, `Integer.MAX_VALUE`, and `Integer.MAX_VALUE + 1` would cover the boundary with a fraction of the cost. The progress-logging cadence (every 10 M) is already a good model for the assertion cadence.
