#YJM1Z: The original code used two simple, independent loops (one for time-series fields, one for score), each with a clear single purpose. The refactored version merges them into one loop with a compound `keepIterating` condition and nested `if/else` branches. While functionally equivalent, this is noticeably harder to read and reason about. If the motivation is to avoid two passes over `plan.output()`, that list is typically small (a handful of attributes), so the readability cost likely outweighs the micro-optimization. Consider keeping the two separate loops for clarity.

#GZRG9: The scoring detection performs two separate traversals of the plan (first over `EsQueryExec`, then over `EsRelation` inside `FragmentExec`). The second traversal does not short-circuit: once `hasScoring` is set to `true`, the `forEachDown` over `FragmentExec` and the nested `forEachDown` over `EsRelation` continue visiting every remaining node. For large plans this is wasted work. Consider adding an early-exit guard (e.g., checking `hasScoring.get()` at the start of each lambda body and returning early).
Additionally, the `EsRelation` check in the second pass duplicates the exact same predicate as `EsQueryExec.hasScoring()` (`a instanceof MetadataAttribute && a.name().equals(MetadataAttribute.SCORE)`). Extracting a small shared utility (e.g., a static method on `MetadataAttribute` or a helper in `EsQueryExec`) would reduce the risk of the two checks drifting apart.
